## Interface and Editing-Pane Follow-Up

This follow-up inspected the actual interface, not just the feature catalogue. Screenshots were viewed in the conversation; this Markdown file is the written reference, not a saved screenshot bundle. No record values, chart styling or report text were changed. The newly opened chart/report editing sessions were discarded at their save prompts.

### 1. Chart Workspace and Element Inspector

**Observed:** Fan-chart viewing has a central canvas, left navigation and a right person browser. Entering Edit changes the title to Edit Chart, presents a page canvas and exposes a Finish action. Selecting the center person replaces the empty right inspector with properties for that chart element.

**Controls inspected:** Full Name; top/bottom margins; fill and border colors; border width; name and secondary fonts; text color; shadow opacity, distance, angle, blur and color; and checkboxes for which events, facts and influential relationships to display. The toolbar includes undo/redo, removal, front/back order, text, lines, images, alignment and realignment. Page setup and pagination controls are visible.

**Health:** Selection-to-inspector behavior worked. Transformation, undo, pagination and export outcomes were not exercised. Some inspector values were truncated in narrow controls, and the name wrapped mid-word.

**KinForge inspiration:** Separate genealogical facts from presentation overrides. A person-selection editor changes records; a chart-element inspector changes presentation. Use readable fields, compact grouped controls and unmistakable mode labels.

### 2. Report Workspace and Document Inspector

**Observed:** A generated Person Report becomes an editable page in Edit Report mode. Clicking its heading visibly selects that text element and opens a different right-hand inspector appropriate to document layout.

**Controls inspected:** Font, style, size, text color, fitting width, horizontal/vertical alignment, line breaks, keeping an element with the preceding object, page breaks, background, border edges/radius, margins and padding. The Add menu offers Text, Image and PDF.

**Health:** Entering edit mode and selecting an element worked. No content was inserted or rewritten. Saving, regeneration semantics and editable export formats remain unverified.

**KinForge inspiration:** Treat reports as structured documents with selection-aware formatting, not screenshots of a chart. Define clearly whether a saved report is a live template or a snapshot, and test what happens when its underlying records change.

### 3. Person Profile and Form Mode

**Observed:** The main person page shows identity and lifespan, parents above, a person browser on the right, and partner/children context along the bottom. Edit changes the page to form mode; Done provides an explicit way out. Empty optional sections become available in editing mode.

**Health:** The form-mode transition worked and the family context stayed visible. The large bottom context pane substantially reduced the visible form area. The fixed header and surrounding panes also consumed considerable vertical space.

**KinForge inspiration:** Retain identity and nearby relationships while editing, with compact or collapsible context panes. Keep the active form's controls reachable without being covered by a floating pane. Use a searchable section list for long records.

### 4. Inline Event Editing and Full Event Detail

**Observed:** Selecting an existing census event expanded an inline editor with type, date, time, description, cause, authority, labels, privacy, notes, place and citation information. Edit Event Details opened a dedicated event page with sections for place, media, notes, citations, stories, tasks, labels and change dates, plus a Show Person action.

**Health:** Both levels of detail opened successfully and retained the selected event. No values were submitted or changed.

**KinForge inspiration:** Provide quick edits in the person's timeline and a focused event editor for evidence-heavy work. Both must edit the same underlying event and preserve a clear route back to the person.

### 5. Historical Date Precision

**Observed:** The date selector has Full Date, Month & Year, Year Only and Date Range modes. The inspected sample retained a year-only value of 1930. Prefix/suffix menus expose uncertainty and qualification terms, including About, Before, After, estimated/calculated variants and multilingual alternatives. The dialog was cancelled without selecting a qualifier or altering the date.

**Health:** The existing precision was represented explicitly. Parsing, calendar conversion, range validation and serialization were not tested.

**KinForge inspiration:** Store date precision and qualifiers explicitly. Do not turn an unknown day or month into an invented January 1 date. Sorting and plausibility rules must respect uncertain dates.

### 6. Tree Selection and People Search

**Observed:** In Interactive Tree, the right pane switches among Edit, Persons and Relatives. Searching for Rose Fitzgerald filtered the people list. Selecting her changed the tree title, selected node and displayed family structure. Selecting John Fitzgerald Kennedy through the relatives list returned the tree to him; the Edit pane then displayed his record. The zoom control was also exercised at a readable editing scale.

**Health:** Search, selection, redraw and the contextual editor worked during this walkthrough. This was a small sample, not a large-tree performance test.

**KinForge inspiration:** Share a stable selected-person identifier across the tree, inspector, reports and research views. Search should locate a person without hiding or altering their family relationships. Preserve tree position sensibly when switching panes.

### 7. Relatives Browser and Focus Context

**Observed:** The Relatives pane grouped the focused person, partner and children. A Lock Focused Person control was visible. When a child was selected, the tree selection changed while the relatives browser still showed the previous person's family context. The lock itself was not toggled.

**Health:** Selecting a relative worked. The distinction between the browser's reference person and the tree's selected person needs clear labelling to avoid confusion.

**KinForge inspiration:** Support an explicit relationship-reference person separately from the active selection when useful. Label both states and provide a clear recenter/reset action.

### Interaction and Accessibility Limits

- The native accessibility tree exposed many fields, menus and sliders, but some visible toolbar and tab controls required screenshot-based targeting. That is an automation observation, not a complete screen-reader assessment.
- Visible text truncation, mid-word wrapping, small chart labels at fit-to-window and the space occupied by persistent panes are risks to address in KinForge's own responsive design.
- No keyboard-only, VoiceOver, contrast-measurement, reduced-motion, mobile-reflow or full accessibility-compliance test was performed.
- These observations support interface inspiration. They do not justify copying MacFamilyTree's branding, icons, illustrations, source code or exact visual skin into KinForge.

### KinForge Acceptance Checks Derived from the Walkthrough

1. Selecting a person in the tree, search or relatives list updates the same person inspector without losing unsaved edits silently.
2. Switching between tree, profile, chart and report retains the appropriate selected person and tree.
3. An inline event and its full editor share one persisted record, with citations and privacy intact.
4. Historical date precision and qualifiers survive save/reload and supported import/export.
5. Chart presentation edits never silently rewrite a person's source records.
6. Report element editing supports usable text/layout controls and a defined live-template or snapshot model.
7. Desktop panes, compact layouts and enlarged text remain usable without overlapping fields or hiding essential actions.
8. Keyboard and assistive-technology users can reach the same navigation, selection, editing and dismissal actions.

### Interface Direction for KinForge

The following are design targets derived from this pass, not newly implemented features:

- **Keep navigation, the main workspace and its inspector distinct.** Use a stable left navigation area, a central tree/list/document, and a context-sensitive inspector where appropriate. A focused editor should be able to use more width when the work becomes evidence-heavy.
- **Keep identity visible without crowding the form.** Use a compact person/event heading and collapsible relationship context. Avoid oversized headings and persistent overlays consuming most of the editing viewport.
- **Provide two editing depths.** Quick inline changes and a dedicated editor must refer to the same record. The deeper editor should expose citations, media, notes and privacy without making common edits cumbersome.
- **Distinguish a source from its citation.** Store reusable source metadata separately from a citation's page locator, notes and certainty. Use explicit actions such as Open Source and Return to Event instead of an ambiguous Edit label that changes the record being viewed.
- **Make evidence navigation bidirectional.** Person -> event -> citation/source and source -> referenced event -> person should be traversable. Preserve selection, and intentionally restore scroll position or focus when returning.
- **Use preview panes for quick context.** Tree and timeline previews can avoid full-page navigation, but they must fit within the viewport and keep close/expand controls reachable. Offer a full view when the content needs more room.
- **Use icons with labels and clear states.** Section glyphs and event pictograms help scanning, but meaning must not depend on color alone. KinForge should keep its own licensed/original glyph system and branding rather than copy MacFamilyTree's artwork or exact visual skin.
- **Adapt to the window.** On wide displays, allow useful side-by-side information. On compact displays or with enlarged text, switch to one active pane, retain a clear back action and prevent floating controls from covering form actions.

### Verification Still Required in KinForge

- One record shared by inline and full editors; correct source-versus-citation ownership.
- Explicit saved/unsaved/error states and protection when navigating away from an edited field.
- Returning to the correct person/event/source, including keyboard focus and sensible scroll restoration.
- Viewport-contained popovers, reachable dismissal, keyboard operation and assistive-technology labels.
- Separate tests of privacy suppression, save/reload, duplicate handling, and evidence preservation in interchange.

## Implications for KinForge

These are implementation and acceptance-test targets, not claims that KinForge already meets them:

1. **Use one connected record model.** A selected person must connect consistently to relationships, events, facts, places, citations, media, stories and tasks across views.
2. **Keep each major workflow distinct.** Tree exploration, structured editing, research, maps, chart composition, report editing and publication need appropriate screens while retaining person/tree context.
3. **Implement genuinely different chart layouts.** Verify the geometry, branch selection, generation limits, repeated ancestors, names, privacy and export output for each required chart.
4. **Separate sources from citations.** Reuse a source while allowing each factual assertion to have its own citation, locator, quotation and evidence assessment.
5. **Make research questions actionable.** Link each question to missing or conflicting data; support answers, deferral and explicit unknown states without inventing family facts.
6. **Make quality findings reviewable.** Show the relevant records and rationale. Account for approximate dates and relationship types, and never silently rewrite a user's tree.
7. **Preserve original media.** Treat crop, color correction and AI transformations as distinct operations with previews and explicit save/revert behavior. A visible checkbox is not evidence that a transformation works.
8. **Treat privacy as part of every workflow.** Private and living-person details must be tested across charts, reports, exports and sharing, not only the editor. DNA, government identifiers, custody/protective-services and criminal-history fields in Ideas.md need especially careful access and disclosure controls.
9. **Test import/export round trips.** Verify relationships, events, citations, media references, dates, encodings and private-data exclusions with real fixtures. The export menu alone is not sufficient.
10. **Do not confuse a provider template with a service.** Storing a DNA record or opening an external search does not deliver DNA analysis, private record access, automatic matching or licensed content.
